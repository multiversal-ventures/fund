# Multifamily ZIP Code Dataset for Three Investment Theses

**This dataset identifies 150+ ZIP codes across 80+ metros for a private real estate fund targeting 10–50 unit Class B/C multifamily properties in the $1–5M deal size range.** Three distinct investment theses are supported: supply-constrained Midwest/secondary markets, refinancing-distressed Sun Belt markets, and structurally low-cost inland markets. The data below draws from CoStar, Yardi Matrix, CBRE, Freddie Mac, BLS, Census ACS, FEMA, and dozens of local market reports. Where exact figures are unavailable, estimates are clearly labeled with methodology. All data reflects 2024–2026 vintages unless noted.

## THESIS 1: The Invisible Supply Wall

The Midwest and Northeast secondary markets are experiencing a structural freeze in small multifamily construction. **Nationally, Midwest construction stands at just 3.4% of inventory versus 6% in Sun Belt markets**, and construction starts dropped 74% from 2021 peaks. The result: occupancy rates above 95% in many metros, rent growth outpacing coastal markets, and virtually no competitive new supply for the next 2–3 years. Class B/C cap rates of **6.5–9.5%** offer substantial yield premiums over institutional gateway markets.

## THESIS 2: The Broken Capital Stack

The multifamily refinancing wall is accelerating. **$310B in multifamily debt matured in 2025, with $162B coming due in 2026 (up 56%) and $168B in 2027.** CMBS multifamily delinquency hit **7.15% in March 2026** — up from 1.84% two years earlier. Nearly 59% of all distressed CMBS loans are past maturity. The 2022 vintage is most exposed, with the highest proportion of loans at DSCR below 1.0×. Per-unit discounts of **15–30%** from 2022 peaks are common across Sun Belt markets that saw massive capital inflows.

Key structural advantage: **Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans are assumable** with lender approval and ~1% assumption fee. Properties with 2020–2021 vintage agency debt at 3–4% fixed rates represent the highest-value acquisition targets — buyers inherit below-market financing that creates instant equity.

Distressed sales peaked at 5.1% of total volume in early 2024, fluctuating around 1.3% by late 2025.

## THESIS 3: The Hidden Cost Moat

The structural operating cost advantage in low-insurance, low-tax inland markets creates **150–430 basis points of hidden yield** invisible in cap rate comparisons. On a $90K/unit property, annual savings of $1,500 in combined insurance and property taxes equals **~167 bps** of effective yield. On a $70K/unit property, the same savings equals **~214 bps**. The moat is strongest where these cost advantages coincide with stable or growing demand fundamentals.

The cost differential is dramatic: **multifamily insurance runs $400–$600/unit/year in inland low-risk markets versus $1,200–$1,800+ in Florida and coastal Texas**. Effective property tax rates range from **0.38% in Alabama** (lowest in the contiguous US) to **1.60% in Texas** and **1.79% in Illinois**.

## Cross-thesis overlap and portfolio construction

Several markets appear across multiple theses, creating compounding investment advantages:

**Huntsville, AL** appears in all three theses — refinancing distress creates buying opportunity (Thesis 2), ultra-low property tax at 0.38% provides structural cost moat (Thesis 3), and once current oversupply absorbs, the defense-driven economy and frozen new construction will trigger supply wall dynamics (Thesis 1). Current 81.8% occupancy represents a **contrarian entry point** for patient capital willing to underwrite a 12–18 month lease-up.

**Oklahoma City** and **Little Rock** bridge Theses 2 and 3 — both have collapsing construction pipelines (starts down 77% and zero respectively), distressed seller activity, and structural cost advantages from low property taxes and insurance. **Boise** bridges Theses 2 and 3 with the strongest combined cost moat ($2,100–$3,200/unit/year advantage) alongside rapid vacancy recovery (-350bps).

**Birmingham, AL** offers the deepest distress (13% vacancy, highest in 25 years) combined with the second-lowest property tax base in the country (0.57%). The pipeline has contracted from 2,500 units to just 520, setting up a supply-constrained recovery by 2027.

For portfolio construction, the highest risk-adjusted returns likely come from blending: **high-yield Thesis 1 markets** (Syracuse at 9.6% cap, Toledo at 9.3%, Rochester at 8.3%) for current income, **recovering Thesis 2 markets** (Boise, Charlotte, Greenville, Chattanooga) for value appreciation, and **Thesis 3 cost moat markets** (Nevada, Wyoming, Idaho metros) for structural margin protection.

### Critical data caveats

All per-unit pricing for 10–50 unit Class B/C properties reflects estimates derived from regional transaction data, broker commentary, and comparable market analysis. Institutional databases (CoStar, Yardi) predominantly track 50+ unit properties, so **small multifamily pricing must be verified through local brokers and actual sales comps**. Cap rates for tertiary markets (Lawton OK, Pocatello ID, Casper WY) have limited transaction data and carry wider confidence intervals. Insurance cost estimates are based on Fannie Mae, RealPage, and Federal Reserve research — property-specific quotes will vary by building age, claims history, and coverage levels. ZIP code selections target known workforce housing corridors with older apartment stock but should be validated against CoStar or PropStream property counts before acquisition targeting begins.
